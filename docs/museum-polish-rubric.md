# Museum Object-Specific Polish Rubric

Museum copy should feel like a concise museum educator guiding a three-minute visit. It should never sound like a content pipeline summarizing metadata.

## Quality Bar

- Each artwork needs at least two object-specific details across the notes: subject, maker, place, use, material technique, date nuance, provenance, series, or collection context.
- The surprising fact must be object-specific and source-backed. It can use the official object record as evidence, but it must not merely say that a record exists.
- The technique note must describe a visible or material feature specific to the object, not a generic lecture about the medium.
- The connection note must connect the artwork to a real cultural, devotional, domestic, technical, art-historical, or collecting context.
- Quiz questions must test looking, memory, and context without relying on raw metadata recall.
- Reinforcements must explain why the answer matters for reading the artwork.

## Quiz Rules

- Observation questions ask about something visible in the image.
- Context questions map to a detail presented in the notes.
- Connection questions bridge to period, use, place, or cultural context without asking for a simple Passport label.
- Distractors should be plausible same-category alternatives, not joke answers or generic filler.
- Answer positions must be deterministic but varied across the pack.

## Forbidden Quality Smells

These phrases are banned in artwork-learning copy and validated in the pipeline:

- `object record`
- `material evidence`
- `maker, material, and date`
- `specific material, date, and object record`
- `worked metal object`
- `photographed subject`
- `painted scene`
- `What material detail helps explain`
- `Which art-historical context best frames`
- `Which period or tradition best helps place`

The earlier self-referential ban remains in force: artwork-learning copy may not mention Passport, threads, today's notes, daily lessons, future visits, collecting paths, or internal game mechanics.

## First-30 Calibration

The first 30 scheduled days are the tone sample. A first-30 record should be either:

- `A ship`: object-specific, clear, and ready as-is.
- `B ship after polish`: shippable after copy polish but worth human curator review later.
- `replace`: the object cannot support a strong Museum lesson without better source evidence or a stronger image.

## Showcase Gate

The active annual pack now requires `review.showcaseTier: A-showcase` for every runtime artwork. A-showcase means the record has official object-page evidence, a visual-quality note, resolved-risk notes, and editor-agent approval recorded as `editor-agent-v1`.

For the prototype pack, editor-agent approval is recorded explicitly as `editor-agent-v1`; it should not be represented as human curator approval.
