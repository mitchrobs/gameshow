# Subset 50-Puzzle Theme Audit

Audit date: 2026-05-16

Source: `src/data/subsetSchedule.ts`

Sample: 50 locked puzzles from the 365-day V1 pack: one representative from each of the 24 editorial theme groups, all 20 holiday overrides, the November 3 `HOPE` birthday pillar puzzle, and the 5 weakest remaining puzzles by the existing `scoreSubsetPuzzleSatisfaction` heuristic.

## Rubric

Scores are `P/X/W/A/F/C`, each 1-5:

- `P` Theme promise: the top-level theme is specific, legible, and player-interesting.
- `X` Axis fit: rows and columns feel cleanly different, not merely generic buckets.
- `W` Word fit: every word belongs naturally to both its row and column.
- `A` Aha quality: solving a line reveals a satisfying category, not just a technical rule.
- `F` Freshness: labels and words avoid overused patterns.
- `C` Calendar tone: ordinary puzzles are date-neutral; special puzzles fit the day respectfully.

## What's Working

- Concrete place theme groups are the pack's strongest format. `Workspaces`, `City Outings`, `Nature Zones`, `School Life`, `Daily Tech`, and `Errands` make both axes easy to understand while still giving the player a pleasing second read.
- The best puzzles use row labels that describe a role inside each column world, not generic nouns. `Power` across Phone/Laptop/Camera and `Workers` across Subway/Museum/Restaurant are better than broad labels like `Objects`.
- Holiday puzzles are strongest when the columns express a real cross-theme lens. `Thanksgiving` works because `Food`, `Events`, and `Gratitude` make the holiday row and comparison rows all readable.
- The 3x3 format is at its best when the center word is memorable but not gimmicky. `HOPE`, `RESOLUTION`, `SCORE`, `LUGGAGE`, and `KEYBOARD` are good center anchors because they feel natural in both axes.

## Issues To Fix First

1. Word-form theme groups are fair but least satisfying. `Starts with B/C/S`, `Ends in T/R/Y`, and `3/5/7 Letters` solve as filters, not themes, so the aha is mostly mechanical.
2. Holiday scaffolds reuse `School` and `Wedding` too often. That keeps the grids solvable, but civic and remembrance dates can feel templated instead of authored.
3. Generic labels are overused across the pack. Current top label counts include `Sounds` 31, `Kitchen` 30, `Office` 29, `Animals` 28, `Storage` 28, `School` 25, `Food` 24, `Motion` 24, and `Music` 24.
4. Phrase/action columns need stricter idiom tests. `Can Be Drawn`, `Can Be Thrown`, `Can Be Stacked`, and similar labels drift when one word only fits by a strained interpretation.
5. High-reuse words are technically under cap but visibly familiar. Top repeated words include `JERSEY`, `MENU`, `PASS`, and `TICKET` at 17 uses; `COSTUME`, `LAMP`, and `PROJECT` at 16; and `CAKE`, `CONDUCTOR`, `HAT`, `LOCKER`, `NOTE`, `RECIPE`, `TENT`, and `TOWEL` at 15.
6. Civic holidays need a higher tone bar than regular hybrids. `Veterans Day`, `Memorial Day`, `MLK Day`, `Presidents' Day`, `Juneteenth`, and `Cinco de Mayo` should avoid filler rows that make the date feel interchangeable.

## Edit Plan

- Word-form puzzles: keep at most one light formal puzzle per month, then replace the rest with semantic theme groups. If a formal puzzle remains, add a second thematic layer so rows are not only `Animals/Foods/Music` and columns are not only spelling rules.
- Holiday scaffolds: replace repeated `Wedding` and `School` rows in civic/remembrance puzzles with rows from the same emotional register: `Community`, `Ceremony`, `Service`, `Memory`, `Family Table`, `Neighborhood`, or date-specific parallels.
- Generic labels: add a quality gate for label counts and label specificity. Prefer `Entry Points`, `Morning Gear`, `Practice Signals`, or `Counter Tools` over `Objects`, `Actions`, `Food`, `Storage`, and `Sounds`.
- Weak cross-fits: for phrase columns, require each word to pass a common-language test in the exact phrase. If "can be thrown a spell" or "can be stacked a poster" needs explanation, swap it.
- High-reuse words: add a freshness pass after generation that penalizes the top 15 repeated words and asks for replacement candidates in the same row/column slot.
